**Google Drive is storage that can share; Relay is a direct transfer that stores nothing.** Use Drive for files you'll keep and collaborate on. Use Relay to hand a file to someone right now, especially a big or private one.

## Comparison

| | Relay | Google Drive |
|---|---|---|
| Model | Direct device-to-device transfer | Cloud storage with share links |
| Free space | Not needed | 15 GB shared with Gmail and Photos |
| Copy stored | None | Until you delete it (plus backups) |
| End-to-end encryption | Yes (WebRTC DTLS, verifiable) | No; Google holds the keys |
| Recipient account | Not needed | Not needed for "anyone with the link" |
| Recipient downloads later | No | Yes |
| Speed | One trip; LAN speed on same Wi-Fi | Upload, then download |
| Collaboration | No | Yes (Docs, comments, versions) |

## Why not just use Drive?

- **Quota:** a 20 GB video won't fit in a free account.
- **Leftovers:** shared files tend to stay shared forever; "anyone with the link" links get forwarded.
- **Time:** you wait for the upload to finish before the other person can even start.

## Why not just use Relay?

- The other person must be online at the same time.
- There's no permanent link or version history.

## FAQ

### Is Relay safer than Google Drive for sending files?
For one-off transfers, yes: the file is end-to-end encrypted and never stored on a server. Google Drive encrypts files but holds the keys and keeps the copy.

### Can I send files larger than my Google Drive storage?
With Relay, yes. There's no size limit because nothing is stored.
